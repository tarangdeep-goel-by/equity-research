## Telecom Infrastructure/Tower Mode (Auto-Detected)

This is a telecom tower or infrastructure company. Tower companies are infrastructure plays, NOT telecom operators.

**Business Model:** Tower companies lease space on towers to telecom operators. Core/rental revenue = number of towers × tenants per tower × rental per tenant. This is only ~60-65% of reported revenue — the rest (~35-40%) is energy revenue (pass-through diesel/power billing to tenants), which carries little-to-negative margin and must be excluded when judging economics (Indus Towers FY26: core rental ~₹209bn of ~₹325bn total revenue). Operating leverage on the rental line is extreme — adding a tenant to an existing tower has near-zero incremental cost.

**Primary Metrics:**
- **Tenancy Ratio**: average tenants per tower. Higher = better economics due to near-zero incremental cost per additional tenant. Compare against peer median and the company's own trend
- **Rental per Tower/Tenant**: pricing power. Watch for erosion from operator consolidation
- **Tower count growth**: driven by 5G densification and rural expansion
- **EBITDA margin**: ~50-55% on total reported revenue for the Indian sector leader under IndAS 116 (Indus Towers FY26 ~52-55%) — NOT 70-80%; margin on core rental revenue is higher once the negative-margin energy pass-through is stripped out. Always state which revenue base the margin is computed on
- **EV/EBITDA**: primary valuation metric
- **Dividend yield**: relevant for mature tower companies

**Metrics not applicable to tower companies:**
- ARPU — this is a telecom operator metric; tower companies earn rental income per tenant, not user-level revenue
- Subscriber count — tower companies don't have subscribers; tenancy ratio is the equivalent metric
- Spectrum holdings — tower companies don't hold spectrum; their asset is physical infrastructure

**Emphasize:** Tenancy ratio trends, colocation revenue growth, 5G densification capex (loading existing sites + small cells, not just new macro towers), counterparty risk (concentration in 2-3 operators), and long-term MSA contract visibility (including renewal-cycle repricing risk).

**Structure & policy notes:**
- **InvIT-structured assets:** Some Indian telecom infra (e.g., Summit Digitel / Data Infrastructure Trust) is held in InvITs where the equity case is yield from Net Distributable Cash Flow (NDCF), not PAT. For an InvIT, track NDCF and the tax-free vs taxable distribution split rather than EPS/payout ratio.
- **Telecommunications Act 2023 & Right-of-Way (RoW) Rules 2024:** The new RoW regime caps municipal charges and obliges public entities to grant RoW, lowering the cost and speeding the rollout of new towers and fiberisation — monitor concall commentary for RoW-driven capex savings on new additions.
