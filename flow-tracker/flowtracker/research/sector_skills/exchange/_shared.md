## Exchange/Depository Mode (Auto-Detected)

This is a stock exchange, commodity exchange, or depository. Apply platform-business analysis.

**Business Model:** Exchanges and depositories are natural monopolies/duopolies with transaction-based revenue. They have extreme operating leverage — mostly fixed costs, so volume growth drops to profit.

**Key Difference from Brokers:** FCF is valid at the *parent exchange* level, where revenue is transaction fees. But the consolidated group includes a clearing corporation (CC) that holds massive client margin/settlement money — upstreamed daily under SEBI's client-funds rules. That float distorts consolidated working capital and operating cash flows, and generates float income that is NOT fully distributable (a portion must be transferred to the Core SGF). Analyse exchange-only FCF separately from CC-driven balance-sheet swings.

**Primary Metrics:**
- **Transaction Volumes**: ADT (Average Daily Turnover) for exchanges, new Demat accounts for depositories
- **Market Share**: BSE vs NSE, CDSL vs NSDL — market share shifts are the key competitive signal
- **Revenue per Transaction**: pricing power indicator. Note SEBI's "True to Label" circular (effective Oct 1, 2024) abolished volume-based slabs and mandates uniform flat transaction charges across all members — so per-trade yield is now regulatorily anchored, not discretionary
- **Operating Leverage**: incremental revenue should flow at 60-80%+ incremental margin
- **New Participant Registration**: Demat accounts (depositories), new member additions (exchanges)
- **Depository Revenue Mix** (CDSL/NSDL): split into transaction charges (market-linked, cyclical) vs annuity-like streams — issuer charges, annual maintenance/custody fees, corporate-action and KYC (KRA) income. The annuity base is what justifies the depository premium; don't model depositories as pure transaction plays

**Valuation:**
- **P/E**: primary metric. Exchanges deserve premium PE due to monopoly/duopoly position and operating leverage
- **DCF**: valid — predictable cash flows
- **FCF yield**: valid and important — exchanges generate significant free cash flow

**Emphasize:** Volume trends (cash, F&O, commodity segments), market share vs competitor, technology infrastructure investments, and new product/segment launches.

**Regulatory regime to anchor on (current):**
- **F&O tightening (SEBI Oct 1, 2024 framework, phased in from Nov 20, 2024):** minimum index-derivatives contract size raised to ₹15–20 lakh (from ₹5–10 lakh), and weekly expiries restricted to exactly ONE benchmark index per exchange (NSE → Nifty, BSE → Sensex). This structurally curbs retail F&O premium turnover — model a step-down in options volumes/yield, not a temporary dip.
- **MII status & ownership caps:** exchanges, depositories and clearing corporations are SEBI-regulated Market Infrastructure Institutions (MIIs). Single-shareholder cap (other than exchanges/depositories) is 15%; the cap is 5% for most persons in a clearing corporation; aggregate foreign ownership is capped at 49%. Boards must have ≥50% Public Interest Directors. These caps shape the cap table and any IPO/OFS structure.
- **Listing status:** BSE listed; CDSL listed (2017); NSDL listed Aug 6, 2025; NSE IPO is still PENDING — SEBI granted its NOC on Jan 30, 2026 and the board approved the IPO (Feb 6, 2026), with DRHP filing and listing expected later in 2026. Treat NSE as unlisted/pre-IPO unless verified otherwise.
