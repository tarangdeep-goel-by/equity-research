## Asset Management Company (AMC) Mode (Auto-Detected)

This is a mutual fund asset management company. Apply AMC-specific analysis.

**Business Model:** AMCs earn management fees as a % of AUM. Revenue = AUM × fee rate. Operating leverage is extreme — costs are mostly fixed, so AUM growth drops directly to profit.

**Primary Metrics:**
- **AUM Growth**: total and split by equity/debt/hybrid. Equity AUM earns 2-3x the fee rate of debt AUM
- **Net Flows**: net new money coming in (gross sales - redemptions). Positive net flows = organic growth beyond market appreciation
- **SIP Book**: monthly SIP flows — most stable and predictable revenue source. SIP book growth rate is a key forward indicator
- **Revenue Yield (bps)**: operating (core management fee) revenue ÷ average AUM × 10000. Use operating revenue, NOT total revenue — total revenue includes volatile treasury/MTM gains on the AMC's own book that distort core fee realization. Blended yields for large AMCs run ~45-50 bps (equity ~55-60 bps, debt ~25-28 bps, liquid ~12-13 bps). Tracks fee realization — should be stable or rising
- **Operating Profit Margin**: top-tier Indian AMCs (HDFC AMC, Nippon Life) run 65-80%+ OPM due to extreme operating leverage; treat anything below ~50% as a red flag for an established AMC

**Valuation:**
- **P/E**: primary metric — AMCs have stable, predictable earnings
- **% of AUM**: Market cap as % of AUM. Indian AMCs typically trade at ~5-15% of *total* AUM (e.g. HDFC AMC ~12-13%), which is roughly ~15-25% of *equity* AUM. Always state which AUM base you are dividing by — equity-AUM and total-AUM percentages are very different
- PE comparison should be against other AMCs, not banks or NBFCs

**Metrics that mislead for AMCs:**
- P/B ratio — AMCs are asset-light businesses where value comes from AUM and fee income, not book assets, so P/B gives misleading comparisons
- EV/EBITDA — not standard for AMCs; P/E is preferred because AMCs have predictable, fee-based earnings
- Working capital analysis — AMCs have minimal working capital needs, so WC metrics are not informative

**Emphasize:** Market share in equity AUM (use QAAUM, not closing AUM), SIP flows growth, distribution reach (IFA vs direct vs bank), B-30 vs T-30 AUM mix, new fund offer pipeline, regulatory impact, and passive/ETF cannibalization risk.

**Regulatory regime (effective 1 April 2026):** SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026 replaced the bundled Total Expense Ratio (TER) with a Base Expense Ratio (BER) that covers only the AMC's fund-management fee. Brokerage/commissions and statutory levies (GST, STT, CTT, stamp duty, exchange/SEBI fees) are now disclosed and charged separately, outside the BER. AUM-based telescopic slabs are retained — the larger the scheme AUM, the lower the cap — so model expense compression as AUM scales. When discussing expense ratios, reference BER (not the old bundled TER).

**Treasury income & mandatory seed capital:** SEBI requires AMCs to invest their own capital (seed capital / skin-in-the-game) in their schemes, so MTM swings on that book flow through Other Income and distort PAT quarter to quarter. Isolate core operating profit from Other/treasury income; attribute Other Income volatility to seed-capital MTM rather than core-business momentum.

**B-30 vs T-30:** B-30 (beyond top-30 cities) assets carry specific SEBI distributor incentives and tend to be stickier retail money; T-30 is the top-30 metros. Track the B-30 vs T-30 AUM mix to read retail penetration, stickiness, and distributor payout leverage.
