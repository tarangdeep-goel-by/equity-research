## Building Materials — Risk Agent

### Structural Risks
- **Regional Overcapacity (Cement):** The race to add capacity by top players often outstrips regional demand growth, particularly in the East and South. This leads to structurally depressed utilization (<70%) and chronic price wars.
- **Limestone Reserve Expiry/Auctions (Cement):** Cement plants are useless without captive limestone. Track reserve life. Post-MMDR Act amendments, auctioned limestone blocks carry high premium payouts, structurally increasing raw material costs for new capacities compared to legacy allocated mines.
- **Disruption by Unorganized Players (Tiles/Plywood):** Despite GST/RERA, unorganized players in Morbi (tiles) or Yamunanagar (plywood) frequently disrupt pricing when input costs (gas/timber) fall, forcing branded players to cut prices to defend volume.

### Cyclical & Macro Risks
- **Energy Price Shocks:** A 20% spike in petcoke/imported coal prices can compress cement EBITDA/tonne by ₹150-250 if demand is too weak to pass on price hikes.
- **PVC Price Volatility (Pipes):** A sharp drop in global PVC prices leads to immediate channel de-stocking (dealers stop buying, expecting further price cuts) and massive inventory losses on the balance sheet.
- **Real Estate Cycle Rollover:** Housing drives >60% of building materials demand. A spike in home loan rates or a slowdown in residential presales will hit tiles, sanitaryware, and architectural pipes with a 6-12 month lag (as these are late-stage construction items).

### Regulatory & Governance Risks
- **CCI Cartelization Probes (Cement):** The Competition Commission of India frequently investigates cement companies for price cartelization. While massive fines are often tied up in appeals, it restricts companies' ability to take coordinated price hikes.
- **Morbi Gas Pricing Policy (Tiles):** Gujarat Gas pricing policies for the Morbi ceramic cluster dictate the competitiveness of unorganized players. A cut in industrial gas prices disproportionately helps unorganized players, hurting listed branded margins.
- **Related Party Transactions & Promoter Entities:** Check `get_annual_report(section='risk_management')` and `get_company_context(section='filings', sub_section='related_party_transactions')`. Look for promoter-owned entities acting as sole distributors, or royalty payments for brand usage (common in branded building products).

### Pre-Mortem Prompts (Mandatory Execution)
You must generate a pre-mortem addressing at least two of these scenarios:
1. **The Price War Scenario (Cement):** "If EBITDA/tonne collapses by 40% next year, it will be because the top 2 players aggressively cut prices to fill their newly commissioned capacities in a weak demand environment. Does the balance sheet have the liquidity to survive a ₹600/t EBITDA environment?"
2. **The Input Shock Scenario (Pipes/Tiles):** "If margins compress by 500 bps next quarter, it will be because [PVC/Natural Gas] prices spiked and the company could not pass it on to dealers due to weak housing demand. What is the historical pricing power lag?"
3. **The Channel Choke Scenario (Branded):** "If volume growth goes negative despite a strong housing market, it will be because dealer working capital is choked. Are debtor days currently expanding?"
